    El Salvador is set to the ISNA method by default on this site because it is widely used in the region. Every method gives the same Dhuhr, Maghrib and Sunrise (apart from small fixed adjustments); they differ mainly in how early Fajr starts and how late Isha begins. If your local mosque or religious authority publishes its own timetable, choose the matching method in the Options box or follow the mosque's timetable for congregational prayers.

    San Salvador does not change its clocks during the year (UTC-6), so prayer times move only with the sun.

    Frequently asked questions about prayer times in San Salvador

    What are today's prayer times in San Salvador?

    They are shown at the top of this page. They are calculated for San Salvador's coordinates and its time zone (America/El_Salvador), and the page refreshes them automatically every day, so you always see the current date.

    Which calculation method is used for San Salvador?

    By default this page uses the Islamic Society of North America (ISNA) method (Fajr at 15°, Isha at 15°), which is the usual choice for El Salvador. You can change the method in the Options box above if your local authority or mosque uses another one.

    How is Asr time calculated in San Salvador?

    The default here is the Standard (Shafi'i, Maliki and Hanbali) calculation, where Asr begins when an object's shadow equals its length (plus the noon shadow). Use the Options box to switch to Hanafi Asr, which begins later.

    Does San Salvador change its clocks for daylight saving time?

    San Salvador does not change its clocks during the year (UTC-6), so prayer times move only with the sun.

    What is the Qibla direction in San Salvador?

    The Qibla from San Salvador is about 56° clockwise from true north (NE), roughly 13,219 km along the great circle to the Kaaba in Makkah. A compass shows magnetic north, which differs slightly from true north, so check your local magnetic declination.

    Can I use these times instead of my local mosque timetable?

    These times are calculated from astronomy and are a reliable guide, but many mosques publish their own adhan and iqamah times, sometimes with fixed adjustments. If your mosque publishes a timetable, follow it for congregational prayers.
